# Changelog

All notable changes to LegendsBelow. Dates are commit dates.

## [Unreleased]

### Added
- **The final floor, the Red Library** (depth 14, below the Archive's resting place; design-dungeon-and-world.md 7.4, new): a hand-made arena instead of a generated floor, a small entrance, a long corridor, and a great hall (`DungeonService::FinalFloor`, `GameEngine::FinalDepth`, `IsFinalFloor`; the banner reads "DEPTH 14 - THE RED LIBRARY"). No objective and no lore book there. Its boss, **the First Archivist** (`enemy_first_archivist`: the Lorekeeper's model, larger and drawn red), waits alone in the hall and fights in three phases:
  - Arcane bolts and a ring under the hero's feet.
  - Below two thirds of its health, enraged: it raises **echoes** of the earlier tiers' guardians in turn, a skeleton warrior, an orc raider, a vampire (up to four at once).
  - Below a third, **unbound** (the boss bar says so): the hall's torches gutter to a fifth of their light until it falls (`TorchSet::Gutter`, `Brightness`, `GutteredBrightness`, `GutterSeconds`; `GameEngine::GetTorchBrightness`), and a second, wider ring around itself opens.
  - **Victory**: its fall wins the run (`RunWonEvent` on the run's bus, handled by `GameEngine::WinRun`). A banner, "THE BELOW IS SILENT", goes before any other waiting (`Fanfare::ShowFirst`) with the codex's last page, **The Red Book** (`lore_final_red_book`, tier 5: no floor holds it; the codex heads it "The Red Library", and `CodexLines` is public). A **way up** opens in the hall instead of a trapdoor (a column of warm light; `IsWayUpOpen`, `GetWayUp`, `WayUpRadius`): stepping into it opens the **victory page** (`MenuPage::Victory`, `VictorySummary`: time below, enemies and bosses slain, gold, falls, pages of the codex; Linger in the Hall (`MenuCommand::Linger`) or Quit to Title). The win is autosaved.
  - **New Game+** (design-loot-and-progression.md 7.3): the victory page's first item, **Descend Again** (`MenuCommand::DescendAgain`, `GameEngine::DescendAgain`), takes the same hero (level, path, gear, bag, gold, companions, restored) down from the first floor of a new Below (a new run seed: other layouts), with a "THE BELOW STIRS" banner. Each cycle (`GameEngine::GetCycle`, `Session::cycle`, saved as `floor.cycle`; old saves load as cycle 0) a floor counts 14 deeper for its enemies' strength, its traps, the legendary lightning, and its finds: rarity, item level, tier, gold piles, objective rewards, and bounties (`GameEngine::ChallengeDepth`); its look, inhabitants, and room sizes go by the depth. The HUD reads "DEPTH 3 - NEW GAME+1", and save slots "depth 3, New Game+1". Playtests: `--cycle 1` (`EngineOptions::startCycle`).
  - **The legacy** keeps victories and the quickest (`Legacy::victories`, `fastestVictory`; the Legacy page lists them), and a first victory unlocks a fifth boon, **Laurels**: new heroes' heirloom weapons are rare (`Boon::Laurels`). `RunStats::bosses` counts the run's bosses; `GameEngine::ItemRarity`.
  - Content (`EnemyDefinition`): `phaseThreeHealthFraction`; `summons`, raised in turn instead of `summon` (`SummonFor`, `IsSummoner`), from `summonFromPhase`; a second telegraphed attack, `phaseThreeSlam` (a new `TelegraphDefinition`; `Slam()` gives the first as one); and a `tint` for the model. The engine: `Enemy::phaseThree`, `Phase()`, `ActiveSlam()`, `summonTurn`; `HudModel::Boss::phase` (was `phaseTwo`); `GameEngine::WoundEnemy` (tools and tests).
  - Below the last tier the floors keep the Archive's look, inhabitants, and boss instead of starting over at the crypts (`ThemeIndexForDepth`, `RosterForDepth`, `BossForDepth`): the resting place at depth 13 is the Archive's now.
- **The fourth tier, the Archive** (depths 10 to 12; design-dungeon-and-world.md 5): dark wood floors and walls of bookshelves (`FloorThemes` "Archive"), a hush of pages and faint chimes (`ambience_archive`, `Scripts/MakeAmbience.py`), pale motes of old magic in the air (`ParticleEffect::ArchiveMotes`). Its inhabitants (KayKit Mystery Monthly, CC0): the **Shadow Blade** (lunges), the **Avian Blademaster** (a tank), the **Thornbow Warden** (a bow, keeps away), the **Lorekeeper** (arcane bolts), the black knights again; its boss, **the Abomination** (Large: a pitchfork, a barn door for a shield, a crushing slam, two phases). **Tier-4 gear** (levels 14, the design's levels 16 to 20 tier): a Runeblade, Dawnbringer, Nightfang, the Starshot Longbow, Astral and Nightweave and Dragonscale armour, the Aegis, ... (11 weapons, 14 armour pieces). The balance bot plays a fourth tier to the fourth resting place (depth 13): every class arrives at level 19 or 20 (Warrior 0, Mage 36, Rogue 42, Cleric 3 deaths).
- **The codex** (design-dungeon-and-world.md 8.3): every floor but the resting places has a book of lore lying in a room (a pale glow while unread); F or d-pad up beside it reads it in a banner, and its page joins the **Codex** (title and pause menus: the pages found, each tier's in turn, the rest as "???"), kept across runs with the legacy (`Legacy::lore`, `legacy.lore`). `Assets/Data/lore.json` (`Content::LoreDefinition`, `ContentDatabase::GetLore`, `FindLore`): sixteen pages, four a tier; `LoreBook`, `GameEngine::GetLoreBook`, `ReadLoreBook`, `WrapText`.
- **Creatures' voices**: the living cry out when hurt (at most every 1.5 seconds) and when they die, each in its voice: a beast's snarl, a brute's grunt, a shriek, a giant's roar (`EnemyDefinition::voice`; `GameSound::Voice*`, `VoiceSounds`; synthesized by the new `Scripts/MakeVoices.py`, CC0, `.wav`; the sound loader takes `.wav` where there is no `.ogg`). Skeletons only clatter.
- **Playtest launches**: `LegendsBelow.exe --class rogue --depth 3 --level 4` skips the title and starts a run there (`EngineOptions::startDepth`, `startLevel`).
- **Xbox readiness**:
  - The run **pauses** (the pause menu opens) when the game loses focus, is suspended, or its controller is unplugged (`GameEngine::OnDeactivated`, `OnSuspending`; a connected gamepad going away).
  - **Screen margin** (Options, 0 to 10% each side, by 1%; kept in the preferences, `PlayerPreferences::uiSafeArea`, 10% by default on Xbox): the whole interface stays inside the TV's safe area (`RenderingSystem::SetUiSafeArea`, `UiOffsetX`/`UiOffsetY`; `GameEngine::GetUiSafeArea`, `SetUiSafeArea`).
  - **No Quit on a console** (`EngineOptions::canQuit`, off for `_GAMING_XBOX`).
  - **Packaging**: `LegendsBelow/PackageLayout.ps1` rewritten: it lays out exactly what ships (the game, `MicrosoftGame.config`, the Agility SDK, `Assets`) from `x64\<Configuration>`, checks the layout is complete (every `Assets\Data` file, models, fonts, sounds, music, menu art, the Store logos), and runs makepkg's genmap and pack in turn. A Release package passes submission validation but for the placeholder Title ID and MSA App ID in `MicrosoftGame.config` (from Partner Center).
- **Floor objectives** (design-dungeon-and-world.md 8.2; `:Objectives`: `FloorObjective`, `ObjectiveKind`, `ChooseObjective`): every floor but the resting places asks one thing of the hero besides the way down, chosen from the floor's own seed. **Slay** a named champion who leads the combat room farthest from the entrance ("Grishnak the Cruel": the room's strongest, with two and a half times its hit points, harder blows, a golden light, three times the experience, and its name across the top like a boss; `Encounter::Promote`, `Enemy::champion`, `title`). **Find** a relic lying in that room (the Lost Tome, the Ancestral Arms, the Miner's Golden Pick). **Free** a captive in a cage there once the room is clear. Or **clear the floor**, every room with enemies. Done, it pays gold and experience by the depth and a find at the hero's feet, with an "OBJECTIVE COMPLETE" banner. The HUD lists it top left, the minimap marks it in green, and saves keep whether it is done (`floor.objective`).
- **The merchant's bounties** (`Bounty`): at each resting place the merchant posts one on a kind of the next tier ("Slay 12 Werewolves below"), shown on the HUD with its count and paid the moment it is done ("BOUNTY FULFILLED"); kept in saves (`party.bounty`).
  - `Assets/Data/objectives.json` (`Content::ObjectiveSettings`, `RelicDefinition`, `ContentDatabase::GetObjectives`): the rewards, the champion's strength, names and titles, the relics and their models, the captive and their cage, the bounty's size and pay.
  - `GameEngine::GetObjective`, `GetBounty`; `DefeatEnemy` and `DamagePlayer` (tools and tests).
- **Each tier its gear** (design-armour-system.md 6.1): tier-2 and tier-3 counterparts of every weapon and armour piece (17 weapons, 26 armour pieces: a Steel Longsword and a Knight's Blade, an Elven Warbow, an Archmage's Robe, a Tower Shield, ...; levels 6 and 10). A drop takes the tier of the depth it falls at, chests and bosses included, and the merchant below a tier sells the next one's: an item becomes its counterpart of the same weapon category and hands, or armour slot and weight (`GameEngine::TierForDepth`, `ForTier`; `WeaponDefinition::tier` and `ArmorDefinition::tier`, read from the key's `_tN`).
- **The healing potion grows with the hero**: 2d4+2 and 1.5 a level (`ConsumableDefinition::healingPerLevel`).
- **Balance, third tier**: four more `BalanceTests` play each class through the ruins and the Frost Golem to the third resting place (depth 10, 45 minutes of game time); at the last pass the heroes reached level 14 or 15 there (the design's third item tier: levels 11 to 15), with Warrior 0, Mage 18, Rogue 36, Cleric 3 deaths (cap 45).
- **The Rogue is tougher**: 7 hit points a level (8 from level 11; were 6 and 7). On the third floor at level 5 the bot's Rogue died 3 to 11 times over six seeds where the others died 0 to 8; with the change, 4 to 6. Flanking and sneak attacks alone did not close the gap. The bot's Rogue still dies most in the first tier, mostly on the third floor at level 4 (before Smoke Bomb).
- **Technical health**:
  - **Coverage**: `Scripts/Test.ps1 -Coverage` runs each test under OpenCppCoverage (our components' sources) and merges the runs into `x64/<Configuration>/Coverage` (Cobertura `coverage.xml` and an HTML report), printing the line rate; CI's new "Debug (coverage)" job installs OpenCppCoverage and uploads the report as the run's "coverage" artifact.
  - **A frame-time budget**: `PerformanceTests` (Release builds) steps a crowded ruins floor (the biggest combat room woken, twelve more enemies, two companions, the shipped models) and holds a step, simulation and render queues, under 4 ms on average and 25 ms at worst (about 0.2 ms here).
  - **Smaller files**: the character screen (rows, input, drawing), dropping items, the path cards, and the banner moved from `PlayerProgress.cpp` to a new implementation unit, `CharacterScreens.cpp`; the engine's tunables and asset names moved from `GameEngine.ixx` to the `:EngineTunables` partition (`EngineTunables`, which `GameEngine` inherits, so they stay `GameEngine::Name`).
  - **The shadow pass skips the far**: only models within the shadow map's reach of its focus are drawn into it (`GpuRenderer::ShadowReach`).
- **Real shadows** from the key light (the strongest of the scene's directional lights): every model, faded walls included, is drawn into a 2048 x 2048 shadow map from the light before the main pass, around what the camera looks at (36 tiles a side, snapped to whole texels so the edges do not crawl), and the model shader filters nine comparisons of it for soft edges. Walls throw shadows across the floors, and the hero, enemies, and props their own; the soft ground shadows stay under them. The dungeon's key light is stronger to match (`GameEngine::DungeonDirectionalLight` 0.45, was 0.3), its shadows full (`SceneLighting::shadows`, 0 turns them off).
  - LBRenderingSystem: the internal partition `:ShadowMap` (`ShadowMap`: the depth texture, its views, `LightViewProjection`, `BeginPass`/`EndPass`), composed by `GpuRenderer` (`BeginShadowPass`, `EndShadowPass`); `ModelPass::Shadow` (depth only, both faces, slope-biased); `ModelPipeline::SetShadow`; the texture heap's last slot is the shadow map's (`GpuResources::ShadowSlot`, `CpuHandle`); `ModelInstanced.hlsli`: `ShadowViewProjection`, `ShadowParams`, `KeyLightVisible`.
- **Knockback** (`Encounter::Knock`, `Enemy::knockVelocity`, `knockSeconds`; `GameEngine::KnockEnemy` for tools and tests): the hero's critical hits throw an enemy back 0.8 tiles (`CritKnockbackTiles`), a boss's blow or a heavy one throws the hero back a tile (`HeavyKnockbackTiles`), and Shield Bash throws its target 1.5 tiles (`SpellDefinition::knockbackTiles`, data). Bosses and the unstaggerable stand fast; walls stop a thrown body; a thrown enemy keeps acting (or stays stunned).
- **Each tier's air**: dust motes hanging in the crypts, mist creeping over the caves' floors, embers drifting up through the ruins, around the hero, on through the tactical pause (`ParticleEffect::CryptDust`, `CaveMist`, `RuinEmbers`; `GameEngine::Atmosphere`, `Atmospheres`; `EffectsSystem::Scatter`; `ThemeIndexForDepth`).
- **Gates, walls, and chests move**: a gate sinks with a slowing ease and grinds up dust; a broken secret wall shudders and falls in on itself; a greater chest keeps its shape and its lid swings open on its hinge (`GameEngine::GreaterChestLid`, `LidSwingSeconds`, `WallCrumbleSeconds`; `RoomState::chestOpenedAt`).
- **Balance through the second tier** (the `BalanceTests` bot grows as a player would):
  - The bot spends its attribute points on its class's attribute (`GameEngine::SpendAttributePoint`, new), takes the first path, wears better finds (`GameEngine::BaseItem`, new), hires a companion and buys potions at the resting place, opens the locked gate with its key and visits the treasure rooms, gives up an optional room that killed it twice, and casts its big spells (those with a cooldown) when ready. Four new tests play each class through the second tier and the Golem Warlord to the second resting place (depth 7, 30 minutes of game time); the first tier has 20 minutes (was 14).
  - **A slower level curve**: 100 (level - 1)^2 experience (was ^1.5), so a hero reaches about level 5 through the first tier and 10 through the second, as the item tiers expect (design-armour-system.md 6.1); before, the bot reached level 20 by the seventh floor. Level 2 and 3 still take 100 and 400 experience.
  - Rooms grow by tiers: two light enemies in the first tier, three in the second, four after; a second heavy one and a second shooter from the sixth floor (were from the fourth). A combat room on floor 5 held ten enemies, four of them werewolves.
  - Softer caves and ruins: the Werewolf 12 hit points, 1d6, slower blows, its lunge every 8 seconds; the Orc Raider's and Vampire's blows slower and a point lighter; the Black Knight's slower. The Necromancer has 58 hit points (was 70): the heroes meet it two levels younger now.
  - At the last pass: first tier Warrior 3, Mage 4, Rogue 24, Cleric 6 deaths; second tier Warrior 3, Mage 19, Rogue 30, Cleric 7. Caps: 15 (the Rogue 35) and 30 (the Rogue 40). The bot's Rogue still sets up no sneak attacks: over six seeds it died 5 to 39 times in the first tier and on two did not get past the Necromancer in time; the other classes cleared both tiers on every seed.
- **Remappable controls** (design-ui-ux-and-audio.md 4): the Controls page has a cursor. Up and down choose a row; Enter or A waits for its new key or button, and Esc or Start keeps the old one. A key another action used is swapped over to it. Moving's keys (WASD, arrows), Esc, and Start are refused, and the last row resets everything. The remapping is kept in the profile (`controls.<action>`) and applied at startup. Each spell has its own row now, and so does each way of turning the camera.
  - `GameEngine::Rebind`, `ResetBindings`, `RemappableActions`, `ControlsCursor`, `IsListeningForBinding`; `MenuModel::ControlRow::binding`, `MenuModel::controlsCursor`, `listening`.
  - `InputSystem::SetBindings`, `ClearActions`, `FirstKeyPressed`, `FirstGamepadButtonPressed`.
- **Allies, second pass** (design-character-system.md 7):
  - **Two companions**, one of each kind: the party holds two (`AllyParty::MaxAllies`).
  - **The Acolyte** (KayKit Cleric, CC0), a new hireling: she mends the worst off of the party, the hero first on a tie, below 60% health (`AllyDefinition::healAmount`, `healCooldownSeconds`, `healRange`; `AllyParty::Heals`, `AllyHeal`).
  - **Orders**: G (d-pad down) switches the party between following and holding the spot the hero stood on (`AllyStance`, `AllyParty::SetStance`). T (d-pad right) sends them after the nearest enemy in sight, and the hero's attack order in the tactical pause names their target too (`AllyParty::Focus`, `FocusTarget`). Both show on the Controls page.
  - **Gear**: a weapon and an armour piece each, given from the character screen's new Allies rows (choose the ally, then the item; what they had goes back to the bag) or taken back (`GameEngine::GiveToAlly`, `TakeFromAlly`, `AllyItems`; `AllyGear`, `AllyParty::SetGear`). Kept in saves.
  - **Statuses and traps** reach them: an enemy spell's slow, root, or burn (`AllyParty::ApplyStatus`, `Ally::statuses`), and spike traps strike them as they strike the hero (`TrapStep::othersStruck`).
- **More enemies** (design-dungeon-and-world.md 5; KayKit Mystery Monthly characters on the same rigs):
  - Each tier its inhabitants (`GameEngine::Roster`, `Rosters`, `RosterForDepth`): the crypts keep their skeletons; the **caves** bring the Werewolf (lunges from up to five tiles), the Orc Raider (a tank that goes berserk below half health), the Witch (Hex Bolt: acid that slows), and the Orc Brute (a Large tank); the **ruins** the Vampire (its blows heal it by half the damage), the Tiefling (Hellfire: fire that burns), and the Black Knight (a Large tank with a cleaving slam). Skeleton crossbowmen shoot in every tier.
  - **The Frost Golem**, the third tier's boss: a cold slam around itself, two phases. Bosses now take turns over three tiers.
  - `EnemyDefinition::lungeRange`, `lungeSpeed`, `lungeCooldownSeconds` (a rush ending in a blow), `lifeSteal`, `rises` (the living stand ready instead of rising from the ground).
- Save slots and the legacy:
  - **Save slots**: Save Game (pause menu) opens three slots of the player's own (`GameEngine::SaveSlots`), Load Game (title and pause menus) every save there is: the autosave, the quick save, and the slots, each with its summary ("Warrior, level 5, depth 7 - 0:42"; `SlotSummary`; saves now record the class, level, and depth). Continue and the death screen's "Load Last Save" load the newest of all of them. `MenuPage::SaveSlots`, `LoadSlots`, `MenuItem::slot`.
  - **Legacy** (design-loot-and-progression.md 7.1, `:Legacy`: `Legacy`, `Boon`, `Boons`): the records of every run (deepest floor, bosses defeated, enemies slain, heroes sent below), kept in the player's profile (`PersistenceSystem::SaveProfile`, `LoadProfile`, `profile.json`), unlock boons for every new hero: Provisions (reach depth 3: an extra potion), Old Coin (defeat a boss: 30 gold), Heirloom (reach depth 7: an uncommon weapon of the class), Veteran (defeat three bosses: a tenth more experience). A new boon is announced across the screen ("LEGACY: PROVISIONS"); the title menu's Legacy page lists records and boons. Only runs being played count (not the one behind the title screen). `GameEngine::GetLegacy`.
- Deeper loot (design-loot-and-progression.md 3):
  - **Item levels**: a rolled item takes the depth it dropped at as its level (`RolledItem::level`, `GameEngine::ItemLevel`); its affixes grow a quarter for each level above the first (`ItemLevelScale`), and it costs a tenth more per level. The character screen shows it ("L5"). Saves keep it ("~base|rarity|affixes|level"; older saves load at level 1).
  - **Attribute, hit point, and mana affixes**: Mighty (+STR), Nimble (+DEX), of the Owl (+INT), of the Hermit (+WIS), Hale and of Vigor (+hit points), of the Well (+mana). `CharacterService::SetGear`, `GetGear`, `GetEffectiveAttributes` (`GearBonus`): gear attributes count for attack, speed, armour class, and spell power but not toward the class caps or the saved attributes; gear hit points and mana stay on top of the class tables through levels, points spent, and loading. The character screen shows "STR 17 (+1 gear)".
  - **Legendary items** (`rarity_legendary`, from the second floor, rarer than epic; `RarityDefinition::legendary`): two affixes and a **power** (affixes of type "Power", `AffixDefinition::power`, `chance`, `powerDamage`, `description`): of Storms (hits may arc lightning to two foes nearby), of the Leech (heals a fifth of the damage dealt), of Winter (hits may chill), of Embers (hits may set the foe on fire), of Thorns (armour: foes striking up close take part of the blow back, arcane). Only the hero's own weapon blows set them off (`CombatEvent::byAlly` marks allies' blows and arrows). The character screen shows the power under the item. `Encounter::DamageEnemy`, `ApplyStatus`; `LootRoller::RollPower`, `Chance`.
  - **Item icons** on the character and merchant screens (`Assets/UI/icon_*.png`, drawn by `Scripts/MakeIcons.py`, CC0: weapons by category, armour by slot, potions, arrows, bolts, keys); long names shrink to fit their column.
  - `GameEngine::MakeItem` (an item from its save description; tools and tests).
- More of the dungeon (design-dungeon-and-world.md 4.3, 6.3, 8.3; the `:Features` partition: `FloorFeatures`, `Gate`, `CandlePuzzle`):
  - **Locked gates** (from the second floor): a dead-end treasure room behind iron bars across its corridor. The **Iron Key** (`consumable_key_iron`, `ConsumableDefinition::isKey`: not used from the bag) falls in the critical-path combat room the branch leaves from once it is clear ("A KEY!"), so the way never locks itself; walking up to the gate with it opens it (the bars sink into the floor); without, "LOCKED - FIND THE KEY".
  - **Secret rooms** (from the third floor): a second dead-end treasure room behind a cracked wall that a blow breaks ("A SECRET PASSAGE!", a cloud of dust); the HUD hints at it nearby.
  - **Puzzle rooms** (from the second floor, `RoomType::Puzzle`, `FloorGenerationParams::puzzleWeight`): four candles by the walls, scrambled; Interact by one flips it and its two neighbors, and when all burn the room's chest opens ("THE CANDLES BURN AS ONE"). Lit candles glow. `GameEngine::TouchCandle`.
  - **Greater chests** behind gates and puzzles (`chest_large_gold`): three rolls of the chest's table, as rare as four floors deeper. Treasure rooms have gold piles about the chest (four in the hidden ones).
  - `WorldMap::SetBlocked`, `IsBlocked`, `Walkable`, `ClearBlocked`: closed-off cells stop bodies, sight, and paths. Gates opened, the key's fall, and the candles are kept in saves. `DropLoot` takes rolls and a depth bonus.
- **Autosave** (`EngineOptions::autoSave`, on in the game; `GameEngine::AutoSave`, `AutoSaveSlot`): the run saves itself when a new game starts, on each floor down, on quitting to the title or the desktop, when the window closes, and when the console suspends the game (not behind the title screen, not while the hero is down); "Saved" shows briefly at the top left. **Continue**, the pause menu's Load Game, and the death screen's load the newest save, autosave or quick save (`LatestSaveSlot`). Save timestamps now carry milliseconds, so saves a moment apart still sort.
- **Dropping items**: on the character screen, X or Delete (controller X) puts the bag item under the cursor on the floor at the hero's feet, the whole stack; it is not picked up again until they have walked away from it (`GameEngine::DropItem`, `DropAction`, `GroundItem::dropped`, `Keys::Delete`). The Controls page lists it.
- **Level ups and paths with ceremony** (`:Ceremony`):
  - A banner across the screen when the hero reaches a level (`Fanfare`, `FanfareCard`): "LEVEL 5" swells in over a dark band edged in gold, the class beneath it, and what was gained appearing line by line: hit points, mana or stamina, attribute points to spend, each ability that opens, and a path to choose. Banners wait their turn; the game goes on under them. `LevelGainedEvent` carries the gains (`hitPoints`, `mana`, `attributePoints`). It replaces the floating "LEVEL UP!", "NEW:", and "CHOOSE A PATH" texts.
  - **Choosing a path** on cards (`PathChoice`, `PathCard`, `GameEngine::PathCards`): "Choose your path..." on the character screen opens a card for each path side by side, with its name on a band of its color, what it is about, and what it gives; the card under the cursor is raised and framed, left and right compare, confirm takes it, back returns. Taking it shows its banner ("PATH OF THE BERSERKER", in the path's color) with a burst of light. `classes.json` paths gain `flavor` and `color` (`SpecializationDefinition`).
- Feel and presentation:
  - **Shadows**: a soft dark shadow on the ground under every character (the hero, allies, enemies, the merchant), and within a torch's light two fainter ones trailing away from the flame, longer and darker the nearer the torch. `RenderingSystem::DrawShadow` (`ShadowRenderData`, `GetQueuedShadows`) draws them in one pass: the particle quads laid flat on the ground with their own shader (`ParticleShadow_PS`, even in the middle, soft at the rim). `GameEngine::ShadowOpacity`, `ShadowHeight`.
  - **Hit-stop and camera shake**: a critical hit by the hero, a boss's blow, and a blow taking a quarter of the hero's health freeze the action for a moment (`EngineOptions::hitStop`, on in the game) and shake the camera, dying away (`GetShake`, `GetHitStopSeconds`); a boss turning enraged shakes it too.
  - **A death screen** (`EngineOptions::deathScreen`, on in the game; `MenuPage::Fallen`): "You have fallen", with the run so far (deepest floor, enemies slain, gold found, time below, falls: `GameEngine::GetRunStats`), and the choice to rise again at the entrance, load the saved game, or quit to the title.
  - **Each tier sounds its own**: a quiet ambience loop under every floor (the crypts' low drone and distant drips, the caves' dripping water, the wind through the ruins; `Assets/Sounds/ambience_*.wav`, made by `Scripts/MakeAmbience.py`, CC0) and footsteps on its ground (stone, earth, weeds: `GameSound::FootstepDirt`, `FootstepGrass`, from Kenney's RPG Audio and Impact Sounds).
- **A controls page** (Controls in the title and pause menus, `MenuPage::Controls`): every action with its keys and mouse buttons and its controller buttons, in sections (exploring, fighting, the tactical pause, screens and menus), scrolling with up and down. The rows are read from the input bindings (`GameEngine::GetControls`, `MenuModel::ControlRow`), so the page follows them; `InputSystem::GetBindings`, `Keys::Name`, and `GamepadButtonName` (Xbox labels) give the names.
- Technical health:
  - **`LB::EventBus`** (LB.Core `:Events`): typed, queued publish and subscribe (`Subscribe<T>`, `Publish`, `Dispatch`, `Pending`, `Clear`; events published while dispatching arrive in the same dispatch, up to `MaxDeliveries`). The engine publishes `EnemyKilledEvent`, `LevelGainedEvent`, and `PathChosenEvent` (`:GameEvents`) on the run's bus (`GameEngine::GetEvents`), dispatched at the end of each step: experience and loot, and the level-up and path fanfare, now answer events instead of being called from the combat code.
  - **Equipment in one place**: the InventoryService's equipment slots are the only record of what is equipped (`GameEngine::EquippedItem`, `WornItems`); the weapon and CharacterService's armour pieces follow from them (`SyncEquipment`). Saves are unchanged.
  - **Path finding for orders**: `WorldMap::FindPath` (breadth first over the cells, leaving the enemies' flow field alone); a move or attack order out of sight walks around the walls, cutting corners where it can see ahead (`GameEngine::GetOrderRoute`, `OrderMoveTo` for tools and tests).
  - **Culling**: floors, walls, torches (with their light's reach), props, and traps outside the camera's view are not drawn (`ViewArea`, `GameEngine::GetViewArea`, `GetWalls`).
- Allies (design-character-system.md 7):
  - **Companions** from `Assets/Data/allies.json` (`Content::AllyDefinition`, `ContentDatabase::GetAllies`/`FindAlly`): the Barbarian (a great axe) and the Ranger (a longbow), KayKit Adventurers bodies. The merchant hires them out on resting floors, one at a time ("Hire: Barbarian", 80 and 90 gold).
  - The `:Allies` partition (`AllyParty`, `Ally`): they follow the hero, go after awake enemies near the hero (shooters keep their distance), strike through the Encounter (their kills give the hero experience), grow with the hero's level, go down at 0 hit points and get up at half health when the fight is over (whole at a shrine; along to every new floor). ### Changed
- Content loading needs `enemies.json`, `consumables.json`, and `loot.json` next to the other data files; enemies name a loot table (`lootTable`), checked against loot.json, whose items are checked against the item files.
- Overlay rectangles are drawn as alpha-blended quads instead of render-target clears; the overlay (rectangles, text, sprites) counts as one draw call.
- `WorldPoint` moved from `LB.Engine:GameEngine` to `LB.Engine:World` (still exported by `LB.Engine`). `Encounter::Configure` takes the `WorldMap` instead of the arena size.
- The model shaders take the material color and emissive per part as root constants and sample a base-color texture (a white texel for untextured materials); the instance stream carries the copy's tint and its skin palette offset.
- `CharacterService` uses data-driven class rules: HP/MP come from the class progression tables (MaxHP = table HP + CON_mod x level; MaxMP = table MP + INT/WIS_mod x level for Mages/Clerics), replacing the hard-coded formulas. Characters can only be created for classes whose rules are configured; the engine configures them from classes.json and runs without a player when there is no content.
- Removed the hard-coded class attribute bonus on creation (classes.json starting attributes are the class template) and the fixed per-level attribute gains. Armour proficiency lists replaced by the armour adjustment model; armour.json `allowedClasses` is no longer cross-checked against them.
- The render target is sRGB (shading in linear space, matching glTF colors); the engine converts its display-space colors, and the default lighting is dimmed with almost no specular.
- fastgltf and simdjson are static (overlay triplets) and built with `/guard:cf /guard:ehcont`; LBRenderingSystem drops `/GL` in Release (corrupt module debug info, LNK4020).

- `LB.Engine` re-exports `LB.RenderingSystem`. DirectXTK12 added to `vcpkg.json` and linked statically into `LBRenderingSystem.lib` through overlay triplets (`triplets/`, `vcpkg-configuration.json`) that keep every other port dynamic; no DirectXTK12.dll ships.

- `vcpkg.json` moved to the repository root so all projects share one manifest.
- LBEngineCore references only the projects it imports (LBRenderingSystem, LBModel.Core).
